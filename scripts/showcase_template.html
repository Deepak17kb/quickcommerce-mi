Price grids hide half a price response

Shelf prices snap to ₹10, ₹50 and ₹100 steps ending in 9, 49 and 99. A naive regression of each follower on Blinkit reads about half the planted response. Simulating the latent prices through each listing's own grid, then matching what the naive regression sees, gets it back. This is the finding that transfers to real data: 75% of real Blinkit prices sit on such grids, so real pass-through estimates would be biased the same way.

Why the pooled hazard curve lies

The chance of a stock-out rises the longer an item stays in stock (a Weibull shape k = 2). Pooled over every listing, the curve flattens and says k = {{K0}}: fragile products run out early, so the long runs belong to sturdy ones. A survival model with a per-listing frailty term, leaving each step free, follows the planted curve (k̂ = {{KV3}}; with the Weibull form, {{K2}}).

Decisions under the fitted dynamics

A simulation engine reads every parameter from the saved models, and must reproduce the status quo before it answers anything. Its answers are about positioning (price rank, cheapest-of-4 share, availability), never demand or revenue: no order data exists. Which stock-outs to fix first?

Same capacity, 1% of each store per snapshot. The model's priority index cuts out-of-stock exposure {{PRI}}%. The intuitive "longest out first" ({{LOF}}%) does worse than random ({{RND}}%): the chance of coming back rises the longer an item has been out, so the longest-out items are about to return anyway.

What this data cannot answer

Demand, sales, orders, revenue. The data is supply-side only: prices, stock, delivery times, offers. The honest substitutes are time until stock-out, restock time and competitive response.

Price elasticity. Needs demand. Deliverable 9 measures how platforms respond to each other, which is a different question.

"Category X sells out faster." One real snapshot gives stock-out prevalence, which mixes how fast items run out with how slowly they come back.

Who leads real pricing. The leader and follower structure was planted in the generator. Recovering it proves the method, not the market.
